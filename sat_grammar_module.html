Chapter 1 — Background

Purpose of This Module

Understanding why this module exists will help you connect the learning to what really matters: your success on the SAT.

The Instructional Problem

Many 11th grade students can recognize parts of speech in a simple sentence — but struggle to apply that knowledge when grammar questions appear inside a longer reading passage, which is exactly how the SAT presents them.

There is a gap between surface-level grammar awareness and the ability to independently identify errors in context. This module is designed to close that gap.

Research Questions This Module Explores

This module was built as part of a research study. The study asks two questions:

  • To what extent do 11th grade students demonstrate improved ability to independently identify grammar errors in passage-based contexts after completing a self-paced learning module?
  • How do 11th grade students describe their experience with immediate corrective feedback while completing SAT grammar practice activities within the module?

What the Research Says

Studies on feedback in learning consistently show that immediate corrective feedback — feedback given right at the point of practice — supports skill building more effectively than delayed feedback (Hattie & Timperley, 2007; Shute, 2008).

This module puts that research into practice. Every question you answer gives you feedback right away, including an explanation of why the answer is correct.

What You Will Learn

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

  • Name and define all eight parts of speech
  • Identify parts of speech within SAT-style passage excerpts
  • Explain how a word's function — not just its form — determines its part of speech
  • Apply parts of speech knowledge to recognize and correct grammar errors
Chapter 2 — Lesson

The 8 Parts of Speech

Learn what each part of speech does in a sentence. Focus on function — how a word is being used — not just memorizing labels.

Why Function Matters More Than Form

The same word can be a different part of speech depending on how it's used. Take the word run:

  • "I run every morning." → run is a verb (action)
  • "I went for a run." → run is a noun (thing/activity)

This is exactly the type of knowledge the SAT tests. You have to look at how the word works in context.

All 8 Parts of Speech

Here are the eight parts of speech, their definitions, and examples.

Noun
Names a person, place, thing, or idea
teacher, Chicago, freedom
Pronoun
Replaces a noun
he, she, they, who, its
Verb
Shows action or state of being
run, is, became, wrote
Adjective
Describes or modifies a noun
bright, small, three, her
Adverb
Modifies a verb, adjective, or adverb
quickly, very, never, there
Preposition
Shows relationship between words
in, on, between, despite
Conjunction
Connects words, phrases, or clauses
and, but, although, because
Interjection
Expresses emotion, often standalone
Oh! Wow. Hey, wait —

SAT-Style Context: What to Watch For

The SAT Writing and Language section will give you a passage and ask you to identify errors or improve word choice. Parts of speech knowledge helps you answer questions like:

  • Should this be an adjective or an adverb? ("The runner moved quick / quickly.")
  • Is this pronoun the right case? ("The award went to she / her.")
  • Does this verb agree with its subject?
  • Is a modifier placed near the word it's supposed to describe?
Chapter 3 — Practice

SAT-Style Practice

Select your answer for each question. You'll get immediate feedback explaining why each answer is correct or incorrect.

Question 1
Read the passage. What part of speech is the underlined word?
The scientist carefully examined each sample before drawing any conclusions.
Question 2
Which word in the sentence below is a conjunction?
Maria studied hard, but she still felt nervous before the exam.
Question 3
The SAT asks you to choose the best word. Which option correctly fills the blank?
The committee made its decision _______, waiting until all evidence had been reviewed.
Question 4
What part of speech is the word between in this sentence?
The rivalry between the two schools had lasted for decades.
Question 5
Which option corrects the grammar error in the underlined portion?
